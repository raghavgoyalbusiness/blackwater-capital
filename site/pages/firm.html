The Firm

Built around responsible capital allocation.

Blackwater Capital is a future-focused investment and asset management brand serving institutions, family offices, founders and long-term private investors. Our purpose is straightforward: to allocate capital thoughtfully, and to do so in a way that can be explained, repeated and defended.

Our purpose

Capital deserves a considered owner.

Capital represents work already done — by a business, a family, an institution or a founder. We treat the responsibility of allocating it as the defining feature of the firm rather than as a service wrapped around a product.

That responsibility shapes practical choices. It informs how carefully we underwrite, how selective we are willing to be, how we describe risk to the people whose capital is at stake, and how we behave in the periods when doing nothing is the correct decision.

Blackwater Capital is designed to think in decades rather than quarters. That intention only means something if it is built into the structure of the firm — how decisions are made, how they are recorded, and how they are reviewed afterwards.

Our principles

Six commitments we hold constant.

Principles are only useful where they constrain behaviour. Each of these is intended to be binding, particularly when it is inconvenient.

Protect capital before pursuing returns
The first question we ask about an opportunity is what could go wrong and what it would cost us. Return expectations are only meaningful once that has been answered honestly.
Think independently
Consensus is information, not instruction. We are willing to hold a view that differs from the market, provided the reasoning is our own and the evidence supports it.
Remain selective
A high bar is only real if it results in a short list. We would rather decline a great many opportunities than accept a mediocre one because capital was available.
Align interests
We seek arrangements where our incentives and those of our investors and partners point in the same direction over the same time horizon.
Operate with integrity
Discretion, candour and consistency between what we say privately and publicly. Where we are uncertain, we say so; where we are wrong, we say that too.
Build enduring partnerships
We would rather have a small number of long relationships than a large number of transactions. Continuity compounds in the same way capital does.

How we make decisions

Evidence first. Then judgement. Then size.

Good investment decisions are rarely the product of a single insight. They are the product of a process that makes it difficult to skip steps, and that separates the question of whether to invest from the question of how much.

  • Every opportunity is written up before it is discussed, so the thesis exists in a form that can be tested rather than performed.
  • The downside case is developed with the same rigour as the upside case, and is stated in terms of what would have to be true.
  • Disagreement is treated as useful. A view that nobody has argued against has not yet been examined.
  • Position size is decided separately from conviction, against the consequence of being wrong.
  • Decisions are reviewed afterwards against what was written at the time — including the ones that worked.

Our culture

Small, focused and quietly rigorous.

Analytical, not adversarial

We argue about evidence rather than about status. The most useful contribution in a discussion is often the one that makes a favoured idea harder to hold.

Discreet by default

We work quietly. Confidentiality is a professional obligation, and our relationships tend to be more valuable than the publicity around them.

Accountable in writing

Positions are recorded before outcomes are known. It is the simplest available protection against remembering ourselves as more prescient than we were.

Long-term vision

An institution intended to outlast any single cycle.

Our ambition is to build a globally minded investment firm with a deliberately narrow definition of what it will own — one whose reputation rests on the quality of its judgement rather than on the volume of its activity.

Reaching that point is a matter of decades, not announcements. It requires consistency in how capital is allocated, continuity in the people making those decisions, and a willingness to grow only where growth improves the quality of the work.

We would rather be understood by a small number of investors and partners who share that horizon than be widely known for something faster.
