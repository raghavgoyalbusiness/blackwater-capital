Investment strategies

Four disciplines. One standard of underwriting.

Blackwater Capital evaluates opportunities across public markets, private equity, private credit and real assets. The instruments differ; the requirement that an investment be understood before it is owned does not.

01 — Public markets

Public Markets

Fundamental investment strategies focused on high-quality businesses, structural growth, and long-term compounding.

Strategic objective
To own a concentrated group of listed businesses whose earnings power we believe is durable, purchased at prices that leave room for error, and held long enough for that earnings power to be reflected in value.
Opportunities considered
Established businesses with defensible positions and identifiable economics; companies undergoing structural change where the market's assessment appears anchored to the past; situations where short-term dislocation has separated price from underlying value.
Investment approach
Bottom-up fundamental research on the business, its industry structure and its management. We form an independent view of normalised earnings and reinvestment potential, define a price at which the risk is compensated, and act only when the market offers it.
Risk management philosophy
Permanent loss of capital is the risk we manage; price volatility is the condition we accept in order to be paid for it. Risk is controlled through underwriting quality, valuation discipline, position sizing and an explicit understanding of what would falsify the thesis.
Typical investment horizon
Multi-year. We expect to hold positions for several years and to judge them over full cycles rather than over quarters.

02 — Private equity

Private Equity

Partnerships with companies where strategic support, operational improvement, and patient capital can create lasting value.

Strategic objective
To build value through ownership — improving how a business operates, competes and is led — rather than through financial engineering or a reliance on exit conditions matching those at entry.
Opportunities considered
Founder-led and family-owned businesses seeking a long-term partner; corporate carve-outs where a division would benefit from focus; companies where a clear operational agenda exists and management is aligned with pursuing it.
Investment approach
Diligence on the business and, equally, on the people. We agree a specific value creation plan before investing, define what success looks like in operational terms, and work alongside management with a structure that supports a multi-year programme.
Risk management philosophy
Conservative capital structures, so that operational progress is not hostage to refinancing conditions. Alignment on incentives and governance agreed at the outset. Downside cases underwritten on assumptions that do not depend on favourable markets.
Typical investment horizon
Long. Private ownership is only worth its illiquidity where the work genuinely requires years, and we expect to be measured over that period.

03 — Private credit

Private Credit

Flexible capital solutions designed around the needs of established and growing businesses.

Strategic objective
To provide capital on terms that are properly compensated and properly protected — lending where we understand the borrower, the security and the realistic path to repayment under adverse as well as expected conditions.
Opportunities considered
Financing for acquisitions, growth and recapitalisations; situations requiring a bespoke structure or timetable that broadly syndicated markets are not well suited to; asset-backed and cash-flow lending where the collateral and covenants are capable of being assessed directly.
Investment approach
Credit-first underwriting: position in the capital structure, quality and enforceability of security, covenant package, and the cash conversion of the underlying business. Documentation is treated as part of the investment, not as an administrative step that follows it.
Risk management philosophy
Seniority and security preferred over incremental yield. Concentration and correlation monitored across borrowers and sectors. Active monitoring after funding, on the basis that early information is the main advantage a lender has.
Typical investment horizon
Medium term, defined by the instrument — typically several years to maturity, with the expectation that the position is held rather than traded.

04 — Real assets

Real Assets

Long-duration investments across infrastructure, property, logistics, energy, and other essential assets.

Strategic objective
To own assets with long contracted or structurally durable demand, where the terms surrounding the asset — not merely its physical presence — support income over an extended period.
Opportunities considered
Infrastructure and infrastructure-like assets; property with defensible locations and credible occupier demand; logistics and distribution facilities; energy and transition-related assets where the regulatory and contractual framework can be assessed with confidence.
Investment approach
Underwrite the contract and the counterparty alongside the asset. Assess inflation linkage, cost exposure, capital expenditure requirements, technological obsolescence and terminal value on conservative assumptions rather than extrapolated ones.
Risk management philosophy
Duration is treated as a risk as well as an attraction. We test what happens when the assumptions embedded in a long contract stop holding, and prefer structures that retain flexibility over those that maximise headline income.
Typical investment horizon
Very long. These are assets we would expect to hold for a decade or more, and underwrite accordingly.
